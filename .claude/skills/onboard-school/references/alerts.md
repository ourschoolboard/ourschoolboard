# Alert threshold and taxonomy

The importance threshold remains intentionally conservative. Prefer draft or
omission over guessing.

Apply this threshold only after completing the document-first review in
`document-review.md`. Conservative publication does not permit conservative
discovery: record every plausible candidate, then reject, draft, or publish it
with a reason.

## Worth flagging

An alert is worth flagging when:

- It affects the day to day lives of students and parents (e.g. phone policy, major schedule change, AI use policy, lunch/bathrooms change).
- It constitutes a high risk for the school's future
- It is an important development to existing events in the school (e.g. a decision to demolish/rebuild a school building)
- It is a major budget change that even people with little/no knowledge of school finance will recognize as highly consequential
- It depicts an emerging trend that will have large effects on the school (declining enrollment, rising absenteeism, etc)
- It involves the hiring/firing/role change of the most important admin (limited to principal, vice principal, and superintendent). This includes signs that roles will change
- Minimum source rule: one collected official record must reasonably support
  the headline and summary. A second source is not required. An agenda or
  official item description may support an alert when the wording clearly says
  the action is proposed, planned, scheduled, or under consideration. Do not
  rewrite a proposal as a completed decision.
- A missing attachment is not disqualifying when the collected official item
  description itself supplies enough detail for the claim.

Do not flag:

- Routine, ceremonial, administrative, and duplicate exclusions that would not matter to families.

## Categories

Use only these current canonical categories:


| Category id  | Label        | Include                                                                                                                                | Exclude                                                                                                             |
| ------------ | ------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `budget`     | Financial    | Budgets, funding changes, grants, deficits, reserves, material contracts, or cuts whose primary impact is financial.                   | Routine warrants and purchases; leadership changes better categorized as `staffing`.                                |
| `curriculum` | Curriculum   | Consequential changes to courses, instructional programs, academic standards, teaching methods, or learning materials.                 | Bell schedules, phones, discipline, and other family-facing operating rules better categorized as `policy`.         |
| `facilities` | Facilities   | Construction, renovation, closure, relocation, capacity, or consequential building-condition projects.                                | Routine maintenance and minor procurement; immediate safety or legal exposure better categorized as `risk`.         |
| `policy`     | Day-to-day   | Rules and decisions that change students' or families' ordinary school experience, schedules, services, or participation.             | Instructional content or academic-program changes better categorized as `curriculum`.                                |
| `risk`       | High-risk    | Material legal, compliance, safety, governance, data-integrity, or operational threats to the school or district's future.             | Merely controversial decisions and ordinary uncertainty without a documented material risk.                         |
| `staffing`   | Admin hiring | Hiring, firing, departure, succession, or major role changes for a principal, vice principal, or superintendent.                       | Routine staff appointments, vacancies below those roles, and broad staffing cuts whose primary impact is financial. |


If no defined category fits, keep the alert in draft and report the gap. Do not
create synonyms or one-off categories during onboarding.

## Severity

- `high`: Immediate or incredibly large impact (e.g. evidence of financial fraud, principal or superintendent resignation, pressing safety risk, major change to curriculum/staffing/discipline, a major blow to student or parent rights, a massive update in a large-scale renovation project)
- `medium`: Very important, but will not overhaul the day to day life of students (e.g. new AI use policy, a new facility being built, a status update in a superintendent search)
- `low`: Routine or unimportant change (e.g. less than 5% of budget allocated to a project, a run of the mill bond issuance, an update on a well-known ongoing project like a charter school opening)

Severity must reflect the documented impact, not how surprising or emotionally
charged the topic sounds.
